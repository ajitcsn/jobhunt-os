# JobHunt OS: business thesis

## The wedge

JobHunt OS is a job-search operating system for ambitious candidates who want to run a targeted search, not spray applications. The product turns a personal assistant's research and writing into a durable, editable workflow: target companies, evidence-backed company intelligence, the right people, tailored outreach, applications, and follow-through.

The customer brings their own Claude subscription. Claude does the expensive reasoning and writing through MCP; JobHunt OS is the persistent system of record and action layer. That keeps the product's AI cost at zero while making the output useful after a chat ends.

## The problem

Most candidates lose momentum between discovering a role and taking the next useful action. A spreadsheet can track applications but cannot connect the company, job description, contacts, research, outreach, follow-up dates, and proof-of-work needed to win a selective role. Job boards optimize for volume; candidates need a system that optimizes for well-targeted conversations and applications.

## Why this can win

The category already proves that candidates pay for organization: Teal positions its tracker as a job-search CRM with contacts, job details, and follow-up support; Huntr combines job tracking, contacts, documents, and metrics. The opportunity is to own the more valuable workflow between a target company and a warm application.

JobHunt OS differentiates with:

- Company-first search: start with a target company and persist the research brief, hiring signals, and outreach angles.
- Relationship-first execution: track a recruiter, alumnus, and hiring leader alongside each company; drafts gain follow-up dates automatically once marked sent.
- Deterministic career guidance: local ATS keyword match, resume hygiene checks, target-role gap analysis, and proof-of-work projects—useful even when the connected assistant is offline.
- Bring-your-own-model economics: no AI provider bills, API keys, or training-data dependency inside the product.

## Focused v1 promise

"By Friday, know exactly which 10 companies matter, who to contact, what to send, and what to do next."

The product should make four loops feel effortless:

1. Add or discover a target company.
2. Turn research into a brief, contacts, and drafts through MCP.
3. Convert an opening into an application with a locally calculated resume-fit review.
4. Return to the command center for follow-ups, deadlines, and weekly momentum.

## What to build next

Prioritize changes that increase repeat usage over a broader job board:

1. Application playbooks: checklist and next action by stage (saved, applying, applied, interview, offer), with interview notes and thank-you/follow-up timing.
2. Company intelligence timeline: retain dated hiring signals, funding/news, product changes, sources, and a confidence label instead of one overwritten research block.
3. Evidence locker: attach the resume version, portfolio link, work sample, and questions to each application so interview prep never starts from scratch.
4. Weekly review: a compact conversion funnel (targeted → contacted → replied → applied → interviewed), stale records, and a single recommended focus.
5. Browser capture, only after validating core retention: save a job description and source URL into Applications in one action.

Avoid building a mass job aggregator or auto-apply bot. Both are expensive, crowded, and pull the product away from its high-intent company-research advantage.

## Pricing & distribution hypothesis

Start with a free personal workspace, then test a $12–15/month Pro plan for unlimited company intelligence history, unlimited application evidence, weekly review exports, and accountability sharing. The core tracker must remain useful for free; paid value should be the compounding history and review layer, not basic access to a job list.

Get the first 100 users through career-switch communities, MBA/alumni job-search groups, bootcamps, and career coaches. Offer coaches a lightweight progress view or weekly export rather than a separate enterprise product. The activation moment is not signup: it is one researched company with three contacts, one sent message, and one tracked application in the first session.
